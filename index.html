Robert Hare's Website on Psychopathy

New Website, 2026

For almost three decades, Without Conscience has been my website dedicated to the explication of theory, research, and societal implications of psychopathy. Because of a complex and frustrating set of technical and other issues, the site has been dormant for several years. Among these issues were Covid-19, the former Webmaster's illness and retirement, and an unannounced termination of support for the Legacy Server that had hosted the platform for years. Although much of the material and links continued to be accessible, I was unable to edit the website, add new content, or read viewer comments and queries. Fortuitous resolution of these issues came during discussions with a relative, Rus Hathaway, who has extensive experience and expertise in Information Technology (IT) and Artificial Intelligence (AI), and was a pioneer in the development of online learning. As the Website Editor, he transferred the original site to a new server for revision and updating. He integrates material that I produce (created content) with material produced by generative AI (curated content). The URL for the new website remains the same: https://www.hare.org.

Objectives

My original plan was to update this website and to include a series of articles on my experiences over the years, under the rubric of Recollections on a Career. This would be a daunting task, given my age and lack of cyberspace sophistication. Luckily for me, Rus envisioned a website that could serve as a proxy for an abridged memoir by presenting the research that my students, colleagues, and I (my group) have conducted over the years, including the development and use of the Psychopathy Checklist-Revised (PCL-R), its predecessors and derivatives. These efforts were part of the more extensive endeavors and achievements of the many hundreds of other investigators and their students across the globe. We provide links to some of these researchers and their articles.

Finally, the scientific, clinical, and applied interest in psychopathy is accompanied by intense public fascination with the topic. Some media and popular portrayals of "psychopaths" are credible and informative, but many more are incredible, sensationalistic, misleading, fanciful, and even bizarre. Podcasts, chatlines, and online quizzes can be even worse. We will have much more to say about these and other issues throughout the pages of this website.

Explore Psychopathy: Dr. Hare's PCL-R and Its Impact

Dr. Robert Hare’s Psychopathy Checklist-Revised (PCL-R) stands as a cornerstone in the research and clinical assessment of psychopathy. Developed with scientific rigor, the PCL-R has established itself as an international standard for identifying the personality, interpersonal, and behavioral traits associated with psychopathy. Its contributions span across mental health, criminal justice, and even corporate environments, helping professionals understand and address the profound effects psychopathy can have on individuals and society.

The PCL-R equips professionals across disciplines to recognize psychopathic traits and their impact, guiding essential decisions in treatment, risk assessment, and public safety. Dr. Hare’s pioneering work with the PCL-R has clarified psychopathy’s complex and often misunderstood nature, bridging clinical research with real-world applications that influence public policy and safeguard communities.

Explore Further: For those who wish to gain a deeper understanding of psychopathy and its far-reaching implications, Dr. Hare invites you to explore the full range of resources available on this website. From foundational research on the Psychopathy Checklist-Revised (PCL-R) to the latest studies and applications across mental health, criminal justice, and corporate settings, there is something here for everyone.

Professionals will find essential insights into assessment tools, treatment considerations, and practical guidance for real-world contexts, while students and researchers can access a comprehensive archive of Dr. Hare’s publications and studies. For those with a general interest in psychopathy, the site offers media content, interviews, and reflections that bring clarity to this complex topic.

Each section of the site is designed to deepen understanding, spark curiosity, and support informed dialogue. Whether you are here as a professional seeking to enhance your practice, a student delving into psychopathy research, or a curious mind drawn to the topic, we invite you to explore, learn, and engage with the resources Dr. Hare has curated.

Support Survivors: Dr. Hare is proud to support the Aftermath: Surviving Psychopathy Foundation (opens in a new tab), a non-profit dedicated to helping those affected by psychopathic behavior. Please consider a donation to assist their efforts in raising awareness, supporting victims, and advancing essential research.

Authorship of Website

This website serves as a platform for Dr. Robert Hare to share his insights, reflections, and research on psychopathy. Sections authored by Dr. Hare are marked with the byline: “by Robert Hare.”

Additional content is curated by the website editor—Rus Hathaway, using generative AI tools to synthesize material from Dr. Hare’s research, publications, and notes. Dr. Hare personally reviews and approves all content to ensure accuracy and alignment with his expertise.

Unless explicitly marked with Dr. Hare’s byline, you can assume the content has been developed by the website editor under his guidance.

The site is intended to inform; it does not provide advice, and generative AI is not used for clinical or forensic diagnosis, classification, risk assessment, release decisions, or treatment evaluation of individuals. See Dr. Hare's note on Analytic Tools.

Thank you for visiting, and we hope this site deepens your understanding of psychopathy.

About Rus Hathaway

In the early 1990s, when most people saw computers as glorified typewriters, Rus Hathaway (opens in a new tab) saw something else: a way to reinvent education. In 1992, he launched CyberHigh (opens in a new tab), Canada’s first fully virtual school. The idea was simple but revolutionary: learning without walls, lockers, or school bells. It was a solution for kids who didn’t fit into the rigid structure of traditional classrooms. Rus stepped away in 1998, but CyberHigh kept running, quietly serving as a lifeline for students who needed a different kind of school.

Hathaway’s path since then has been anything but conventional. He earned a Master’s in Education Leadership and worked with Naverus, an aerospace navigation startup, where he designed training programs for their client, Southwest Airlines, teaching pilots to master flight automation systems. (Because, as it turns out, flying a 737 isn’t something you just pick up as you go.) Later, he lent his skills to Mammoet, crafting industrial documentation for a company that moves impossibly massive objects. Eventually, he found his way back to education, designing learning experiences at MacEwan University, the University of Alberta, and the University of Washington.

By the time MOOCs—massive online courses—took off, Hathaway was already ahead of the curve. He’s built some of the most impactful ones on Coursera, including Software Product Management (opens in a new tab), Software Design Architecture (opens in a new tab), Astro 101: Black Holes (opens in a new tab), and Bugs 101: Insect-Human Interactions (opens in a new tab) (University of Alberta), and Machine Teaching for Autonomous AI (opens in a new tab), Designing Autonomous AI (opens in a new tab) (University of Washington). His latest, Building Webpages with HTML & CSS wasn’t just another line on his résumé—it gave him the tools to build this website. Call it his first foray into web development and a project rooted in preserving a legacy.

Today, Hathaway is embracing generative AI. Not because it’s trendy, but because he sees it as the next frontier in education. For Rus, it’s not about teaching people what to think—it’s about teaching them how to think in a world that’s changing faster than ever. If that means shaking things up again, he’s ready. After all, it wouldn’t be the first time.
